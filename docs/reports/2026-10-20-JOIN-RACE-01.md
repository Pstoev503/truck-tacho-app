## Decision and transaction order

The handler uses the first invite lookup only to identify the driver. It then locks and refreshes the driver row, locks and refreshes the invite row, and only after both locks are acquired obtains fresh time and evaluates driver activity and invite use/expiry. A conditional `UPDATE ... RETURNING` claims the invite before old-token revocation and new-token issuance. This matches the driver's deactivation lock order. Rejecting an inactive driver or an expired invite raises the same generic `404`; transaction close rolls back any claim. The rate-limit Python lock is released before all database waits.

New API regressions cover five concurrent claims with persisted invite/token state, normalized aliases, distinct drivers in one company, separate companies, loser quota and the 11th `429`, same-driver claims through distinct invites, rollback followed by successful retry, and controlled post-lock expiry and deactivation. The latter two fail against `d505199` with `200` and pass at `ce8ae95`.
