## Decision and transaction order

The handler uses the first invite lookup only to identify the driver. It then locks and refreshes the driver row and checks activity under that lock. Next it locks and refreshes the invite row, obtains fresh time after both locks are acquired, and evaluates invite use/expiry. A conditional `UPDATE ... RETURNING` claims the invite before old-token revocation and new-token issuance. Creation/renewal now locks and refreshes the driver before changing invite rows, matching join and deactivation order. Rejecting an inactive driver or an expired invite raises the same generic `404`; transaction close rolls back any claim. The rate-limit Python lock is released before all database waits.

Eleven API regressions were added over the supplied 196-test source baseline. They cover one-code contention, five simultaneous claims with before/after invite and token state, normalized aliases, distinct drivers in one company, separate companies, loser quota and the 11th `429`, same-driver claims through distinct invites, rollback followed by successful retry, controlled post-lock expiry and deactivation, and concurrent join/renewal lock ordering. The validity assertions fail against `d505199` with stale `200`; the renewal race deadlocks against `bf8960b`; all pass at `7ce7dde`.
